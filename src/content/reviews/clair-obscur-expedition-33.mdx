This is one I sat on for longer than I would like to admit. It's funny looking back at when I first found the trailers and going "neat, it's like Mario & Luigi." A week into release it was hailed by some as game of the decade, and I happen to have PC Game Pass. Admittedly, I was expecting it to somehow change me more than Xenoblade has. I, as well as many others, may have fallen into the trap of measuring the game against everything said about it rather than the game itself. I doubt my opinion would have changed all that much even if I went in knowing nothing, however. The game, as well as the story around its making, simply spoke to many people more strongly than it did to me.

When the game was over I struggled to define how I actually felt about the experience. If I could describe it, it was as if I felt obligated to give the game a stronger response; perhaps because of how loud the conversation around it already was. It's very likely I'll declare this my top game of the 2025 year, but even so this may not be as positive as my other articles. Still, as I'm writing this I've come to believe this is a game any fan of the JRPG genre will benefit from experiencing, even if they don't exactly enjoy it. Despite all its flaws, it led to me truly understanding what it is about this style of experience I'm actually looking for going forward.
