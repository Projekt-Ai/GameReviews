<div class="bf-prose">

Through the beginning chapter, your attention is diverted away for just a moment to make something very clear: You are being watched. And whatever it is, it's huge. Deciding that it's not worth worrying about, you press on. Climbing up a long staircase, you're met with a gargantuan machine. With no other path forward, you approach in hopes it's somehow inactive... until the multiple lights brighten on it and the sounds of an engine roaring to life can be heard. You have no choice but to kill it same as the diggers.

P later calls these Elders, and surmises one rules each level. With it being the size of a small building, a four-inch chainsaw feels less threatening.

<div class="bf-prose">
The pure awe of the initial scene had me just wanting to run towards it, and of course I got run over immediately. But in trying to find a way underneath the thing, I came to a worthwhile revelation: You can parry the truck.

If you can pull that off it stops moving for awhile, plenty of time to sneak under and begin climbing. As you climb the boss becomes the first of many checks on if you truly understand the game's motorslice mechanic, as mastering it will end up the only path to completion.
</div>

{/* ── PROSE: THE ARGUMENT ─────────────────── */}

<div class="bf-prose">

The Dump Truck reflects the initial view of the megastructure: It's huge, the only way forward is up, and there's no going back to where you started. Like any good first boss, it sets the standard the others will follow — find a grapple point, then use every skill the previous levels have trained you in to rip through each weak point.

Admittedly, once you know how the fight works, it becomes really easy. I refought it just for the sake of putting a certain game's boss music over a recording, and it took less than five minutes for a successful attempt. But I believe that's more to the credit of the game's ability to push the player into learning its mechanics than a failure to challenge them. That said, if a player desires a true challenge, they'll find too much of it soon enough.
</div>

<div class="bf-prose">

The chapter leads with ensuring you know something huge is watching you. By the end of the fight, that's what stuck with me. Not that it was big, but that it was paying attention the whole time.

In hindsight, it was pretty standard all things considered. But it's what had me looking forward to the rest of the game; and to that end it has fulfilled its purpose.
